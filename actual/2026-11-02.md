## 3. Render time — measured, not changed
A 1-script Comic Cover video, headless Chrome for Testing on this
8-core box with no GPU (software H.264), app.js patched in flight:
- baseline: 85 s for 735 frames; drawing the frames took 0.29 s of it.
  It is all the encoder: ~115 ms a frame.
- encoder `latencyMode: "realtime"`: 80 s (−6%).
- 24 fps instead of 30: 56 s for 492 frames (same per-frame cost, fewer
  frames).
- four encoders in parallel, one per quarter of the timeline, chunks
  stitched in order: 119 s. Slower; Chrome's software encoders don't run
  side by side. Dead end, recorded so it isn't tried again.
- 720×1280: 41 s for 675 frames (~61 ms a frame, about half).
So on a machine without hardware encoding the only real levers are size
(halves it) and frame rate (−20%); both change the output. Nothing
shipped; the boss was given the numbers and asked for theirs.

## Later: the boss's phone render, and two look changes
The boss rendered a short on their phone: quick (so no 720p option), but
the frame was a wide page image cover-cropped to a sliver, its text cut
at both edges. Two changes, frames sent to the boss first, "that looks
fine", deployed together:
- Wide and square images (aspect over 0.8) draw whole on a rounded card
  above the caption, over the blurred copy, with a slow zoom; tall
  images keep the full-frame look. Tested with ShelfCall's 1200×630
  social card, a 630×630 crop and the 440×925 app screenshot.
- "The page, on a phone": a scene's picture can be the page itself,
  photographed at phone width by /api/shot (as "Put yourself in it"
  does), in a phone-shaped window above the caption, scrolled to the
  element sharing two or more word stems with the scene's line (one
  shared word landed on short labels like "Sales"), else a step down the
  page per scene, drifting 180 CSS px over the scene. Scenes without a
  picture default to it. First photograph ~5 s, then cached a day; cap
  10 pages a day per account. The render says "Photographing the page on
  a phone…" and fails with the reason if it can't; the still falls back
  quietly.
